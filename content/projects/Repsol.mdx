In the environment of large energy corporations like Repsol, risk applications are not merely administrative tools; they are **critical corporate systems** that support strategic decisions and ensure strict regulatory compliance.

My work focused on the **ERMT (Enterprise Risk Management Technology)** ecosystem, developing robust solutions under **.NET Core 3.1 and .NET 6** (C# and Visual Basic), designed to manage the inherent complexity of enterprise, financial, operational, and regulatory risk.

## 1. The Energy Risk Ecosystem

Development in this sector implies understanding and modeling highly complex business domains. Unlike conventional applications, here the focus is on **data integrity, traceability, and simulation**.

### 🛠️ Enterprise Risk (ERM)
The core of corporate strategy. We developed systems for:
*   **Corporate Risk Catalogs:** Dynamic inventory of threats.
*   **Impact × Probability Assessment:** Algorithms for automatic generation of **Heatmaps** and risk matrices.
*   **Tracking of KRIs (Key Risk Indicators):** Continuous monitoring of key indicators.
*   **Control and Mitigation:** Management of action plans and internal/external audits.
    *   *Examples:* Refinery shutdown risk, critical supplier dependencies, reputational risk.

### 📉 Market and Financial Risk
Given the volatility of the energy sector, these applications require high mathematical precision:
*   Price Management (Crude, Gas, Electricity) and Exchange Rates.
*   Calculation of **Value at Risk (VaR)** and Stress Testing.
*   Scenario Simulation for Hedging (Derivatives, Swaps).
*   Direct integration with trading systems (ETRM).

### ⚙️ Operational Risk
Critical systems for the physical and operational safety of industrial assets:
*   Management of **HSE (Health, Safety & Environment)** incidents.
*   Predictive vs. corrective maintenance of assets (turbines, plants).
*   Failure analysis and prevention of technological obsolescence.

### ⚖️ Regulatory Risk and Compliance
Regulatory compliance is "heavy" and essential in Energy:
*   Support for global regulations (SOX, ESMA, CNMC).
*   **Total Traceability:** Every action is recorded for audits.
*   Management of Non-Conformities and mandatory regulatory reporting.

## 2. Technical Anatomy of a Risk Application

From an engineering perspective, these applications are distinguished by very demanding non-functional requirements.

**a) Structured Data Management**
It is not enough to save the current state. Systems of **full historical versioning** (Temporal Tables, Event Sourcing) are implemented to answer: *"What was the exact status of this risk on February 14th two years ago?"*.

**b) Complex Approval Workflows**
Workflows are not linear. They include complex state machines:
> *Creation -> Lead Validation -> Committee Review -> Audit Approval -> Action Plan -> Follow-up.*
All managed with granular control of deadlines, alerts, and escalations.

**c) Critical Corporate Reporting**
The data presentation layer is vital for Top Management:
*   Generation of executive reports (massive PDF/Excel).
*   Interactive dashboards integrated with **Power BI / Tableau**.

**d) Enterprise Integrations**
The system does not live in isolation; it acts as a data orchestrator that talks with:
*   **SAP** (Finance and Assets).
*   **ETRM** (Energy Trading Systems).
*   Corporate Directories (AD/SSO) for identity management.

## 4. Development Reality: Myths vs. Reality

It is important to understand the context of these projects to properly value the technical profile.

*   ❌ **It is not "Magic AI":** Although it is talked about, the base is deterministic logic and robust statistics.
*   ❌ **It is not "Design-First":** The priority is not a beautiful UI, but a **functional UI** that allows absolute traceability.
*   ❌ **It is not Pure Cloud-Native:** Often they are hybrid ecosystems due to data regulations and legacy systems.
*   ✅ **It is Pure Governance:** They are slow systems to change because every change has massive legal and operational implications.
